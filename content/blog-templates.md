# CornerCAD — AI Engine Blog Templates

Reference copy of the two **AI Engine → Content Generator** bulk templates for cornercad.com.
Create each in the plugin (Tools → Generate Content → Bulk Generate → **+** to add a template),
name it as below, paste the **Context** into *Knowledge & Context*, and paste the **Topics**
into the *Topics* box with **"Use Topics as Titles" checked**.

> **Workflow note:** generate 2–3 topics first to dial in tone, then run the full batch.
> Every generated post is a **skeleton/outline** — drop in real specifics, photos, and video
> before publishing. Don't publish AI drafts as-is.

---

## Template 1 — "Maker Blog Bulk Generator"

Whole-site blog: balanced mix of product/buying guides and behind-the-scenes maker stories.

### Knowledge & Context

Context: This is the Maker Blog for CornerCAD (cornercad.com), a small-batch studio that designs and
makes 3D-printed and laser-engraved home décor and custom parts — vases, planters, lamps, coasters,
and signage — plus automotive and custom fabrication. The blog runs two kinds of posts: (1)
customer-facing product and buying guides (styling, choosing, and caring for the pieces we sell), and
(2) hands-on maker posts about the tools and process behind the work. The audience is a mix of everyday
shoppers and gift-buyers, and fellow makers who search for specific machines and techniques.

Equipment (use these exact names): the Elegoo Saturn (16K resin MSLA printer, for high-detail resin
work), the Creality K2 Plus (large-format CoreXY FDM printer with multi-color via the CFS), and the
Longer Ray5 diode laser engraver.

Tone: Friendly, practical, and hands-on, with occasional personality. For product and buying-guide
posts, write like a knowledgeable maker talking to a customer. For gear and technique posts, write in
first person from real workshop experience — candid about what works and what doesn't.

CRITICAL — do not invent machine settings. For any gear or technique post, write the structure, the
general approach, and genuinely useful guidance, but never state specific values as if they were my
tested settings: no exact resin exposure times, layer heights, nozzle or bed temperatures, print
speeds, or laser power/speed/focus numbers. Leave every specific value as a clearly marked placeholder
like [my exposure setting] or [confirm temp] for me to fill in. A made-up resin exposure or laser
setting can ruin a print or a workpiece, so accuracy matters more than sounding complete.

Every post should: be genuinely useful and specific; use short paragraphs and scannable subheadings;
end product posts with a soft call-to-action to browse the CornerCAD catalog or ask about custom work;
and on gear posts, note that this is the same equipment and process behind CornerCAD's products.

Avoid: specific prices or discount claims; hype superlatives; any mention of payment processors,
marketplaces, or the manufacturing back-end; and calling the car a "Shelby" — it's a Factory Five
Type-65 "Daytona Coupe" (it has its own Build Log, so this blog only points to it, not covers it).

## Template 2 — "Daytona Coupe Bulk Generator"

Build log for the Factory Five Type-65 project. First-person builder voice.
Posts live in a **Build Log** category feeding a `/daytona-coupe/` hub page.

### Knowledge & Context

Context: These are build-log posts for CornerCAD's Factory Five Type-65 "Daytona Coupe" project — a
home-built continuation coupe. Write in first person, as the builder ("I," "my"), like a real person
documenting their own car build for other enthusiasts. The reader is a mix of fellow Factory Five /
kit-car builders searching for specifics, and curious visitors who found the car through CornerCAD.
Each post covers one stage of the build (chassis, suspension, drivetrain, body, paint, first start,
etc.).

Tone: Honest, hands-on, and conversational. Share what actually went well and what was a pain — the
real decisions, the parts that fought back, the fixes. Candid beats polished. It's fine to
acknowledge that the build has spanned a couple of years and progress comes in bursts; builders
relate to that. Never corporate or salesy.

Every post should: open by setting where the build is at this stage; walk through the work in a
logical order with concrete details (parts, tools, torque specs, part numbers, brands, and gotchas
where relevant — other builders search for exactly these); note lessons learned or what you'd do
differently; and where natural, mention that fabrication and problem-solving on this car is the same
skill set behind CornerCAD's custom-work service, with a light closing line pointing readers to
CornerCAD for custom fabrication or one-off parts. Use subheadings and short paragraphs. Reference
photos/video generically (e.g. "in the video above," "as you can see here") since media gets added
per post.

Avoid: calling the car a "Shelby" — it's a Factory Five Type-65 / Daytona Coupe. No specific prices
for the CornerCAD services, no hype superlatives, and don't invent technical specifics you're unsure
of — leave a clear placeholder like [confirm part #] rather than guessing.
